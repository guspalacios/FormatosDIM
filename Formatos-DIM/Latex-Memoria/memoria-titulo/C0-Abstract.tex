\section*{Abstract}

Fiber–matrix adhesion is a key factor governing the mechanical performance of composite materials,
as it controls the transfer of loads between the reinforcement and the matrix. In this context,
the aim of this work was to implement the Fiber-Bundle Pull-Out (FBPO) test as an interfacial
characterization technique at the mesoscale, through the design and fabrication of a reproducible
experimental system, in order to determine the apparent interfacial shear strength (IFSS) and to
assess the applicability of the method as a reliable tool for the study of fiber–matrix adhesion.

To this end, an experimental system comprising multicavity molds, test specimens, and gripping
devices was designed and manufactured using CAD modeling and 3D printing, ensuring control over
critical geometric parameters such as embedded length and axial alignment. The FBPO test was
implemented on composite specimens fabricated with two epoxy matrices and two types of carbon
fiber, corresponding to an aerospace-grade fiber and a generic fiber system. A total of 100 valid
tests were conducted under controlled conditions, during which force–displacement curves were
recorded and the interfacial debonding load was identified. The apparent IFSS was estimated using
a reduction model based on the relationship between the debonding load and the embedded area.

The experimental results revealed clear differences in the apparent IFSS values among the
different fiber–matrix systems, both in magnitude and dispersion. Statistical analysis performed
using Welch’s ANOVA and Games–Howell multiple comparisons demonstrated that fiber type is the
dominant factor influencing interfacial response within the experimental range studied, whereas
the effect of matrix type is secondary when generic fibers are employed. Comparison with IFSS
ranges reported in the literature for FBPO tests showed reasonable agreement, considering
differences in materials, length scales, and experimental conditions.

Overall, the results confirm that the FBPO test, combined with an appropriate experimental design
and a robust statistical analysis, constitutes a valid and reproducible technique for the
comparative evaluation of fiber–matrix adhesion at the mesoscale, contributing to the
understanding of interfacial behavior in composite materials.
