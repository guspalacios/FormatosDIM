\section*{Abstract}

Fiber--matrix adhesion is a determining factor in the mechanical performance of composite
materials, as it governs the transfer of loads between the reinforcement and the matrix. In this
context, the objective of the present work was to implement the Fiber-Bundle Pull-Out (FBPO)
test as an interfacial characterization technique at the mesoscale, through the design and
fabrication of a reproducible experimental system, in order to determine the apparent
interfacial shear strength (IFSS) and to evaluate the usefulness of the method as a reliable tool
for the study of fiber--matrix adhesion.

To this end, an experimental system was designed and fabricated, including multicavity molds,
test specimens, and gripping devices, using CAD modeling and 3D printing. This approach made it
possible to ensure control of critical geometric parameters such as embedded length and axial
alignment. The FBPO test was implemented on composite specimens manufactured with two epoxy
matrices and two types of carbon fiber, corresponding to an aerospace-grade system and a generic
fiber. A total of 100 valid tests were carried out under controlled conditions, during which
force--displacement curves were recorded and the interfacial debonding load was determined. The
apparent IFSS was estimated using a reduction model based on the relationship between the
debonding load and the embedded area.

The experimental results revealed clear differences in the apparent IFSS values among the
different fiber--matrix systems, both in terms of magnitude and dispersion. Statistical analysis,
performed using Welch’s ANOVA and Games--Howell multiple comparisons, showed that fiber type is
the dominant factor in the interfacial response within the experimental range studied, whereas
the effect of matrix type is secondary when generic fibers are employed. Comparison with IFSS
ranges reported in the literature for FBPO tests showed reasonable agreement, considering
differences in scale, materials, and experimental conditions.

Overall, the results confirm that the FBPO test, when accompanied by an appropriate experimental
design and a robust statistical analysis, constitutes a valid and reproducible technique for the
comparative evaluation of fiber--matrix adhesion at the mesoscale, contributing to the study and
understanding of interfacial behavior in composite materials.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} fiber-matrix adhesion; interfacial shear strength (IFSS);
Fiber-Bundle Pull-Out (FBPO); composite materials; interfacial characterization; mesoscale.
