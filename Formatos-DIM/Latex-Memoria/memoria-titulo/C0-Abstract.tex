\section*{Abstract}

Fiber--matrix adhesion is a key factor governing the mechanical performance of composite
materials, as it controls the load transfer between the reinforcement and the matrix. In this
context, the objective of the present work was to implement the \textit{Fiber-Bundle Pull-Out}
(FBPO) test as a mesoscale interfacial characterization technique, through the design and
fabrication of a reproducible experimental setup, in order to determine the interfacial shear
strength (apparent IFSS) and to assess the usefulness of the method as a reliable tool for the
study of fiber - matrix adhesion.

To this end, an experimental system was designed and manufactured, including multicavity molds,
specimens, and gripping devices, using CAD modeling and 3D printing. This approach ensured
adequate control of critical geometric parameters, such as embedded length and axial alignment.
The FBPO test was implemented on composite specimens fabricated with two epoxy matrices and two
types of carbon fiber, corresponding to an aerospace-grade fiber and a generic fiber. A total of
100 valid tests were conducted under controlled conditions, recording force--displacement curves
and determining the interfacial debonding load. The apparent IFSS was estimated using a reduction
model based on the relationship between the debonding load and the embedded interfacial area.

The experimental results revealed clear differences in apparent IFSS values among the different
fiber - matrix systems, both in terms of magnitude and experimental dispersion. Statistical
analysis, performed using Welch’s ANOVA and Games-Howell multiple comparisons, showed that the
fiber type constitutes the dominant factor in the interfacial response within the experimental
range studied, whereas the effect of the matrix type was secondary when generic fiber was used.
Comparison with IFSS ranges reported in the literature for FBPO tests showed reasonable agreement,
considering differences in scale, materials, and experimental conditions.

Overall, the results confirm that the FBPO test, when combined with an appropriate experimental
design and a robust statistical analysis, constitutes a valid and reproducible technique for the
comparative evaluation of fiber - matrix adhesion at the mesoscale, contributing to the study and
understanding of interfacial behavior in composite materials.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} fiber - matrix adhesion; interfacial shear strength (IFSS);
Fiber-Bundle Pull-Out (FBPO); composite materials; interfacial characterization; mesoscale.
